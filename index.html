Forensic Entomology

Forensic entomology applies insect and arthropod biology to legal investigations. Blow flies and other flies can colonize remains early, while beetles and other arthropods may become more prominent later. Development and succession can contribute to minimum post-mortem interval estimates, but interpretation depends on environmental and case-specific factors.

01 • Fresh

Early post-mortem period; insect activity may begin quickly when remains are accessible.

02 • Bloat

Microbial and physical changes progress; insect colonization develops.

03 • Active decay

High insect activity; larvae can be important for development-based estimates.

04 • Advanced decay

Different arthropod communities become increasingly relevant.

05 • Dry remains

Beetles and other taxa may provide succession and ecological evidence.

Important: This site is an educational reference, not a tool for independently determining time of death or other forensic conclusions.
